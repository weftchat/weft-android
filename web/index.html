Security

Private by design, not by promise.

Weft protects what you say and the traces around it: who you talk to, when and from where.

No identity to hand over

No phone number, no email, no username. Your identity is a key created on your phone — there is no account for anyone to seize.

Metadata stays dark

Messages are end-to-end encrypted and routed through two private relays, so no server knows who talks to whom, or when.

Duress PIN & decoy

Forced to unlock? A second PIN opens a harmless decoy profile that looks completely real.

Emergency wipe

One long press erases every chat and key on the device. Ten wrong PINs do the same, automatically.

Leaves nothing behind

Disappearing messages by default, no screenshots, GPS stripped from photos, incognito keyboard and tracker-free links.

Hard to block

Your own relays, .onion addresses and automatic relay rotation keep Weft reachable when networks are censored.

USB guard

Plug in a data cable and Weft locks instantly, wipes its keys from memory and asks for your passphrase. A copied phone yields only encrypted noise.

Panic passcode

Type your panic passcode and Weft seems to open as normal, while it silently erases every chat, contact and key.

Best on GrapheneOS

Pair Weft with GrapheneOS to block the USB port while locked, wipe the whole phone with a duress PIN and auto-reboot when idle.

How it works

Three steps. No sign-up.

Install and set a PIN

Weft creates your keys on the device. Nothing about you is ever sent to a server.

Connect in person or by link

Add contacts by scanning a QR code or sharing a one-time link. There is no user directory to search.

Talk freely

Chats, groups, files and calls travel encrypted through private relays — yours or ours.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is Weft available now?

Weft is in development. A private beta for Android and GrapheneOS is coming first. Join the list above and we will write once, when it is ready.

Why no iPhone version?

We start where privacy is strongest: GrapheneOS and Android, with zero Google services. iOS may come later.

How is Weft different from Signal?

Signal is excellent, but it requires a phone number. Weft is built on the SimpleX protocol, which has no user identifiers at all — not even random ones.

Can you read my messages or hand over my data?

No. Messages are end-to-end encrypted and we have no accounts, phone numbers or contact lists to hand over. Relays only see encrypted queues with no owner.

Is it open source?

Yes. Weft is licensed under AGPLv3 and the code will be public, so anyone can audit it.

How is Weft funded?

By donations. No ads, no investors, no data sales. Donating never unlocks features.
